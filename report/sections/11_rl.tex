\section{Reinforcement-Learning Layer}
\label{sec:rl}

\subsection{Research question and hypothesis}

The weight sweep of Section~\ref{sec:experiments} is used to tune one scalar. An even more
ambitious question is whether the \emph{choice of search move} can be learned. CPSolver's
neighbourhood selector selects a type of move at each step---change a time, change a room, swap
two classes, ask for a suggestion---from a set of moves, by using fixed probabilities. The
assumption of this part of the project was that a policy based on the state of the search could
perform better than these fixed probabilities, and thus achieve a lower rate of student conflict
for the same spending.

A success criterion was predetermined: a learned policy would be deemed a success if it decreased
the student violation rate by at least 25\,\% compared to a tuned baseline policy, at the same
wall-clock budget, on a held-out term. The negative result reported below is meaningful because
the threshold was fixed prior to running the experiment.

\subsection{Design invariant}

The layer is realised as a \texttt{NeighbourSelection}, CPSolver's extension point for move
selection. This is important for safety, as no placement is written in the layer. Every candidate
move comes from a stock CPSolver generator and is then accepted or rejected by CPSolver's Great
Deluge acceptance criterion~\cite{dueck1993deluge}, the same as in a normal solve. The hard
constraints do not need to be re-enforced by any extra filter; they are enforced by the same
filter as in the absence of the layer, and \texttt{Basic.DisobeyHard} remains \texttt{false}. An
informed policy may thus make the search slower or less effective, but may not render the
timetable illegal.

\subsection{The decision process}

The search is formulated as a Markov decision process (MDP)~\cite{sutton2018rl}, in which an agent
sees a state, takes an action, and gets a reward based on the state that follows. Here the agent
is sampled only once every $K = 50$ solver iterations, rather than at every iteration, so the cost
of the inference itself is not significantly higher than the cost of the search itself.

\paragraph{State.} A real vector with 48 dimensions, as listed in \texttt{StateEncoder.NAMES},
grouped as: conflict features (violation rate, pair rate, intensity, concentration, the share of
classes involved in a conflict, pairs per conflicted student); a ten-bin histogram of conflict
load across the day; search-progress features (improvement gap, recent slope, progress, acceptance
rate, idle and null fractions, churn); solution features (assigned ratio, unassigned fraction,
hard violations, and normalised time, room, distribution, too-big-room, useless-half-hour,
back-to-back and broken-pattern criteria); utilisation features for rooms and instructors; and a
one-hot record of the previous operator.

\paragraph{Actions.} Eight operators, six of which are stock CPSolver generators and two of which
were developed for this project and biased to sample classes in conflict:

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{The eight operators available to the policy.}
\label{tab:ops}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}clL{8.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Id} & \textbf{Operator} & \textbf{Description} \\
\midrule
0 & \texttt{TimeChange}          & change a class time in the same room \\
1 & \texttt{RoomChange}          & change room, same time, same class \\
2 & \texttt{TimeAndRoom}         & relocate one class to a different time or room \\
3 & \texttt{LectureSwap}         & swap the class locations \\
4 & \texttt{Suggestion}          & CPSolver's suggestion search; repairs unassigned classes \\
5 & \texttt{StudentSwap}         & exchange a student between parallel courses \\
6 & \texttt{BiasedTimeChange}    & time change, sampled towards conflicting classes \\
7 & \texttt{BiasedStudentSwap}   & student swap, sampled towards conflicted students \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Operators which cannot apply are masked: operator 4 is masked when the solution is complete,
because suggestions only repair unassigned classes, and operators 5 and 7 are masked if there is
no alternative section for the corresponding class. An operator that returns no move is masked for
the rest of the macro-step and the policy is re-queried with the new mask. The two student-swap
operators can be permanently disabled through \texttt{Rl.DisabledOperators}, since the batches are
assigned administratively. This keeps the layer consistent with the pinning policy of
Section~\ref{sec:setup}.

\paragraph{Reward.} A shaping term for the incumbent solution, based on potential,
\[
  \Phi = -C - 0.5\,U - 5\,H - 0.05\,O ,
\]
where $C$ is the student violation rate, $U$ the unassigned fraction, $H$ the hard violations and
$O$ the normalised soft objective. The step reward is the difference in $\Phi$ between successive
macro-steps, minus a fixed penalty of $10^{-4}$ per macro-step to prevent stalling.
Potential-based shaping~\cite{ng1999shaping} is employed so that the reward signal is dense but
does not change the ordering of final solutions.

\subsection{Architecture}

Figure~\ref{fig:rl} shows the two modes in which the layer is executed. Each worker is a virtual
machine with a resident solver, providing reset and step operations via gRPC, a remote-procedure
call protocol. A number of such workers are used to train Maskable PPO (Proximal Policy
Optimization~\cite{schulman2017ppo}, a policy-gradient reinforcement-learning algorithm, with
action masking~\cite{schulte2017maskable}) on the Python side. Training consists of three
EnvServer workers, each with a JVM and a solver, driven by the Python Maskable PPO trainer via
gRPC Reset and Step calls, and a behaviour-cloning prior from SHADOW trajectories. In production
there is no Python and no network: the trained policy is exported to ONNX (Open Neural Network
Exchange, a portable model format) and evaluated within the solver's own JVM, with a timeout and a
fallback to the stock selector if the model evaluates too slowly. UniTime or the headless CPSolver
calls \texttt{RlNeighbourSelection} every $K = 50$ iterations. It passes the 48-dimensional state
vector and the operator mask to ONNX Runtime within the same JVM (about 0.08\,ms per forward
pass), which returns an operator id. If inference times out or takes a long time, the stock
selector takes over.

\subsection{Evaluation design}

Five arms were defined to isolate an effect of learning from an effect of re-weighting or from a
different search strategy.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Experimental arms.}
\label{tab:arms}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}clL{7.4cm}l@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Arm} & \textbf{Role} & \textbf{Selector} & \textbf{Comparator} \\
\midrule
B0 & untuned baseline & \texttt{FixCompleteSolutionNeighbourSelection}, stock weights & native \\
B1 & tuned baseline   & as B0, but the student-conflict weight is increased            & native \\
B2 & strategy control & \texttt{SimpleSearch} Great Deluge, student swap, suggestions  & native \\
L  & online learning  & the layer with LinUCB over the eight operators                & lexicographic \\
R  & frozen policy    & the layer with the exported ONNX policy                       & lexicographic \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Arm L is a linear method based on LinUCB~\cite{li2010linucb}, from the family of
upper-confidence-bound algorithms~\cite{auer2002ucb}. It is an online algorithm, not a neural
network, and a cheap learner that does not require any pre-training. The PPO-trained policy is
the policy in arm R, which is frozen to ONNX. SHADOW is a sixth mode that runs the default
selector while recording the behaviour it \emph{would} have chosen, and provides the trajectories
for behaviour cloning.

\subsection{What was put into place, assessed and not determined}

The layer is full. Its key premise is not.

\paragraph{Implemented.} 25 Java classes and 11 Python modules: a neighbour-selection wrapper, all
eight operators, the incremental conflict oracle, the 48-feature encoder, four policy back-ends,
the headless runner, the gRPC environment and a synthetic instance generator. It recovers a full
recompute over more than 200 batches of random moves, and the exported ONNX graph is the same as
the PyTorch model.

\paragraph{Based on synthetic data, only.} For 120 classes, 600 students, 6 rooms, 30 seconds and
one seed, the pipeline runs end to end, with an average of 100 macro-steps per second per worker,
0.08\,ms for an ONNX forward pass, and a PyTorch--ONNX deviation of $2\times10^{-6}$. All arms tie
at $0.133$ and the behaviour-cloned policy is inferior, at $0.267$, having collapsed on one
operator. This is a pipeline check, as noted in the project's own notes and in this report.

\paragraph{Based on the actual data.} Training was conducted for 100{,}000 timesteps on three
workers on the Spr6 instance, and a policy was exported. The only bake-off on a real instance is
for B0, B1 and B2, and that instance, when loaded, was conflict-free, so it could not have
separated the arms.

\paragraph{Not established.} No claim is made that the learned policy is better than the
baselines. There is no held-out term, and arms L and R have never been run on a COEP instance.

\subsection{Outcome: the weight sweep, not the learned comparison}

Arm B1 was created to determine whether the objective was under-weighted. On this fixed instance,
raising the weight takes the median from 0.0414 to 0.0030 at weight 2, the best weight on disk
(Section~\ref{sec:experiments}). The 25\,\% bar was a different test, a learned policy against a
tuned baseline on a held-out instance, and that comparison was not run.

This is a discovery, not a mistake. On this instance and this budget the residual conflicts were
due to the objective being priced, rather than to how the search explored, and a tuned stock
solver will suffice. This does not put the oracle, the encoder or the headless runner out of use:
they are what facilitated the controlled comparison, and they will be available if a larger
instance fails to yield to the weight.

\subsection{Risks}

Inference cost is limited by macro-stepping, the ONNX timeout and the fallback. The unassigned
term in $\Phi$ discourages a policy that cheats by leaving classes unassigned, since it results in
a decrease in the gradient for any episode in which the assigned ratio has fallen by less than
five points. No generalisation claim is made, as over-fitting to one term is not handled.
